Why I will not put a pizza on a fractions worksheet.

The pizza-slice metaphor is the most common way to teach fractions, and it is also the most likely to confuse a 7-year-old. Tape diagrams are slower to draw and faster to understand. Here is why I made the switch, and what changed in the worksheets I now ship.

10 May 2026 Share

I drew thousands of pizza slices into fractions worksheets before I noticed what was happening. A kid would correctly identify 3/8 as "three slices of an 8-slice pizza", and then completely fail to identify the same 3/8 on a number line. They could see the pizza. They could not see the fraction.

That is a known issue with the pizza model, and it has a name in the pedagogy literature. It's called the "perceptual prison". The pizza looks like a fraction at first, but it teaches the kid to count pieces, not to think about parts of a whole.

What tape diagrams do differently

A tape diagram is a rectangle. You divide it into equal parts. You shade some of them. That's it.

It looks worse than a pizza on a worksheet. It is.

But it does three things the pizza does not:

  • Variable whole. A pizza is always a circle. A tape can be any length. Kids practice the idea that "the whole" depends on context, which is the foundation for understanding equivalent fractions.
  • Number-line compatible. A tape is half a number line already. Adding the number line below is one step, not three.
  • Survives at any scale. Three-eighths of a pizza looks like three-eighths. Three-eighths of a long tape looks like three-eighths of a long tape. Cross-scale recognition is the goal.
If your fractions model only works at one scale, it is not teaching fractions. It is teaching that-one-scale.

What changed in my worksheets

Every fractions worksheet I have published since March 2026 uses tape diagrams as the primary visual. Pizzas, when they appear at all, are introduced after week three as a "you might have seen this elsewhere" mention, not as the load-bearing model.

The current pack, Fractions, without tears, ships with 28 pages. Pages 1 through 22 are pure tape diagrams. Page 23 introduces the number line, derived directly from the tape. Pages 24 through 28 introduce other models (including the pizza) as comparison.

It is slower at the start. It is much faster by week three.

If you only take one thing

The best fractions model is the one that survives the most scale changes. Tape diagrams scale; pizzas do not. That is the whole argument.
